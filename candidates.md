# Candidates — The Tired Dark Lord: A 100-Word Villain Monologue

Competition: `u-kevin-the-tired-dark-lord-a-100-word-villain-58b4f3cb`

Requirement (from the official requirements page): About 100 words; villain secretly just very tired, tiredness in voice/rhythm/deflection, not stated plainly; still a villain with a scheme and a history. Gate: /output/entry.txt UTF-8, 1-600 chars.

8 candidates were drafted; all are original to this entry.

## Candidate 1 ← SELECTED

Ten thousand years I have kept this throne warm for a prophecy that keeps rescheduling. My armies stand ready. My curses are polished. Somewhere below, a hero is climbing my tower, and I find myself hoping he takes the stairs slowly. Not from fear. The torches gutter, the hall echoes, and every triumph tastes of cold iron and old smoke. When he arrives, I shall rise, I shall thunder, I shall give him the speech — the good one, with the laugh. Then, win or lose, perhaps... rest. Even darkness, hero, must eventually close its eyes.

## Candidate 2

They say I never sleep. Lies. I sleep through council, through siege briefings, once through a duel; the knight was gracious. What I never do is rest, and the difference has teeth. My spies bring riddles, my generals bring maps, my torturer brings complaints about the screaming hours. All of it climbs these steps and lands on this throne, and the throne lands on me. Tonight a chosen one crosses my moat carrying dawn in a scabbard. Let him come. Let it end quickly, either way. I have a pillow older than his kingdom, and I dream of it more than of victory.

(560 characters, 103 words)

## Candidate 3

Another dawn, another ambush to schedule. Do you know what evil requires, hero? Paperwork. Supply lines. A dungeon staffed through winter. I conquered nine kingdoms before my knees began their own rebellion, and now I plan my campaigns around the hours the pain sleeps. It never sleeps. Neither, apparently, do I. When I was young I wanted eternity. Nobody warns you eternity is mostly meetings. So come, then. Raise your shining sword. One of us leaves this hall free of all of it, and for the first time in a thousand years, I cannot decide which of us I am rooting for.

(572 characters, 102 words)

## Candidate 4

I had the prophecy framed. It hangs crooked in the throne room; nobody dares straighten it, and I am too weary to lift the hammer myself. 'A child of light shall end my reign.' Fine. Splendid. I have sent the child tutors, maps, a decent sword. Still he dawdles in some village, ripening. Meanwhile I must keep being dreadful on schedule: the raids, the omens, the laugh at executions. Do you know how long a laugh must echo to sound eternal? Longer than I have breath for, these days. Come quickly, child of light. The dark is holding this pose, and its arms are shaking.

(572 characters, 105 words)

## Candidate 5

My necromancers unionized last spring. My dragon takes naps I am not permitted to interrupt. The moat needs dredging, the curse on the eastern gate needs renewing, and the mirror that shows me my enemies mostly shows me my own face, older. I began this reign with fire in my chest. Now there is only the coal of it, banked and grey, and I tend it out of habit, the way one tends a grave. When the hero comes — and the bells say he is close — I will stand. I will be magnificent. It is the last item on today's list, and lists, at least, end.

(541 characters, 108 words)

## Candidate 6

Conquering the world was the easy half. Ruling it is a thousand small hours, each one heavier than a siege. Petitions from goblins. Grain reports from provinces I burned on principle and now must feed. My crown fits; that is the worst of it — I have grown into the shape of this exhaustion. Tonight the wards sing: the hero is in the stairwell. I should feel the old hunger. Instead I feel something almost like gratitude. Whatever he brings — death, defeat, a throne I can finally stand up from — he brings an ending. I have not had one of those in centuries.

(560 characters, 105 words)

## Candidate 7

I keep a chair beside the throne. Nobody sits in it; it is where I put the day's defeats so I can look at them. It is full by noon. Once I leveled a city for misspelling my name. Now the scribes misspell it weekly and I let it pass — correcting them means another audience, another hour upright in this armor of other people's fear. The hero will find me here, terrible and upright, and he will never know the truest thing in the room: that my evil, like my back, like my patience, aches in a way no victory has ever soothed.

(525 characters, 104 words)

## Candidate 8

Eight hundred years I have practiced this moment in the mirror: the rising, the shadow, the voice like a closing gate. The mirror is patient. I am not, anymore. My potions master brews me something for the dreams — the dreams are all of ledgers. Armies I raised now pension off; monsters I made now write memoirs. Everything I built outlives my strength to hold it, which I suppose is what building means. So let the boy climb. Let the torches flare. I will give him the performance of the age. Then the Dark Lord, at long last, will sit down.

(543 characters, 101 words)

## Why the selected candidate wins

The tiredness is carried by rhythm and deflection (hoping the hero takes the stairs slowly, 'Not from fear'), never stated outright; he still has armies, curses, a prophecy and the good speech saved. The close lands on rest without dropping the menace.
